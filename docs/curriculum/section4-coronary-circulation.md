# Coronary Circulation

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Coronary Circulation.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
The coronary circulation is like a delivery truck that can only unload packages when the engine is off: because the contracting heart muscle squeezes its own blood vessels during systole, the heart can only receive its own nourishment (coronary blood flow) during diastole.

### 3. Basic Concept
Coronary circulation delivers oxygenated blood to the myocardium. It is unique because 80% of left ventricular coronary blood flow occurs during diastole, and it is regulated primarily by metabolic factors.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Left Main Coronary Artery (divides into LAD and Circumflex) and Right Coronary Artery. They originate from the sinuses of Valsalva just above the aortic valve.

### 5. Physiology
Normal coronary blood flow is 250 mL/min (5% of cardiac output). The myocardium has a high oxygen extraction ratio (70-80%), meaning oxygen delivery can only increase by increasing blood flow.

### 6. Mechanism
Local Metabolic Autoregulation: When cardiac workload increases, oxygen consumption rises, producing local vasodilators, primarily Adenosine and Nitric Oxide (NO). These dilate coronary arterioles to increase blood flow.

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) is characterized by atherosclerotic plaques. When narrowing exceeds 70%, oxygen delivery during exercise falls below demand, causing angina pectoris.

### 11. Disorders
1. Angina Pectoris: Transient myocardial ischemia causing chest pain. 2. Myocardial Infarction: Complete occlusion of a coronary artery leading to necrosis.

### 14. Mnemonics

### 15. Viva Questions
1. Why is the subendocardium most vulnerable to ischemia? **Answer**: It experiences the highest compressive forces during systole, limiting its perfusion window compared to epicardial layers.
2. What is the primary metabolic vasodilator in the coronary circulation? **Answer**: Adenosine.

### 16. MCQs
1. Most of the blood flow to the left ventricle occurs during:
   * A) Isovolumetric contraction
   * B) Rapid ejection phase
   * C) Diastole
   * D) Atrial systole
   *Answer*: C (During ventricular systole, myocardial compression squeezes coronary vessels, preventing flow. Perfuson occurs during diastole.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient experiences crushing chest pain that radiates to their left arm during exertion, relieved by sublingual Nitroglycerin. **Analysis**: This is stable angina. Exertion increases oxygen demand. Nitroglycerin releases Nitric Oxide, causing systemic venodilation (reducing preload/workload) and coronary vasodilation.

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: What is the normal myocardial oxygen extraction ratio?
  **Back**: Approximately 70-80% (very high compared to skeletal muscle's 25%).
- **Front**: Which artery is the main branch of the Left Main Coronary Artery?
  **Back**: The Left Anterior Descending (LAD) and Left Circumflex (LCx) arteries.

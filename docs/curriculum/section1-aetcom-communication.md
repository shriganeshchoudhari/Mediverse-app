# AETCOM Module 1.1: Doctor-Patient Communication

## 1. Introduction
Communication is a core clinical skill in medical practice. Effective doctor-patient communication improves diagnostic accuracy, patient compliance, and clinical outcomes, while lowering litigation rates.

---

## 2. Core Communication Models
*   **The Calgary-Cambridge Guide**: Structures the consultation into:
    1. Initiating the session (establishing rapport)
    2. Gathering information (active listening)
    3. Physical examination
    4. Explanation and planning
    5. Closing the session.
*   **SPIKES Protocol**: The standard model for delivering bad news:
    *   **S**etting up the interview
    *   **P**erception of the patient
    *   **I**nvitation to share
    *   **K**nowledge sharing
    *   **E**mpathy validation
    *   **S**trategy & Summary.

---

## 3. High-Yield Communication Techniques
*   **Active Listening**: Using non-verbal cues (nodding, eye contact) and open-ended questions ("Tell me more about what you are feeling...").
*   **Empathy Validation**: Actively recognizing and acknowledging patient distress ("I understand this is a very difficult time for you...").

---

## 4. Practice Viva Questions
1.  **What is the SPIKES protocol used for?**
    *Answer:* It is a structured protocol used to deliver bad news to patients or their relatives.
2.  **Define active listening.**
    *Answer:* The practice of fully focusing on, understanding, responding to, and remembering what the patient is communicating, utilizing both verbal and non-verbal techniques.

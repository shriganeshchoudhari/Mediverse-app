# Pulmonary Function Tests

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Pulmonary Function Tests.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
PFTs are like inspecting a house's ventilation system: spirometry checks how fast and how much air can blow out of the vents (distinguishing a clog/asthma from a shrunk room/fibrosis), while diffusion capacity checks how well air leaks through the duct filters into the rooms.

### 3. Basic Concept
PFTs assess lung volumes, capacities, and flow rates to differentiate obstructive and restrictive lung diseases.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Conducting airways, alveolar space, chest wall, and respiratory muscles.

### 5. Physiology
- **Lung Volumes & Capacities**: Standard reference values include Tidal Volume (TV $\approx 500$ mL), Vital Capacity (VC $\approx 4.6$ L), and Residual Volume (RV $\approx 1.2$ L). 
- **Interpretive Strategy (ATS/ERS Standards)**: Rather than relying on fixed cuts (e.g., FEV1/FVC < 70% or TLC < 80% predicted), clinical interpretation should compare values to the Lower Limit of Normal (LLN) or Z-score (defining abnormality as $<-1.645$ standard deviations below predicted values based on age, sex, height, and ethnicity). A fixed ratio of $<0.70$ can overdiagnose obstruction in older adults and underdiagnose it in younger cohorts.
- **Ventilatory Defects**: 
  - *Obstructive*: FEV1/FVC ratio is below the LLN.
  - *Restrictive*: FEV1/FVC is normal or elevated, but Total Lung Capacity (TLC) measured via plethysmography is below the LLN (confirming restricted lung expansion).

### 6. Mechanism
- **Airway Caliber & Resistance**: In obstructive disease (such as asthma or COPD), expiratory flows are limited by narrowed conducting airways or loss of elastic recoil, decreasing FEV1. In restrictive disease (such as interstitial lung disease or chest wall deformities), reduced compliance limits lung expansion, leading to proportional reductions in both FEV1 and FVC, keeping the ratio normal.

### 10. Clinical Correlation
- **Reversibility Testing**: Bronchodilator responsiveness is defined as an increase in FEV1 or FVC by $>12\%$ and $>200$ mL after administering a short-acting beta-agonist (e.g., Albuterol). While a highly significant, fully reversible pattern is highly suggestive of Asthma, reversibility is *not* a absolute diagnostic divider from COPD. Patients with COPD can show significant acute bronchodilator reversibility, and patients with severe, chronic asthma can develop fixed airway obstruction. Differentiation requires integrating clinical history, onset age, exposure history (smoking), and DLCO.

### 11. Disorders
1. Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD): Characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation that is not fully reversible. 
2. Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF): Progressive fibrosing interstitial pneumonia characterized by decreased lung compliance, restrictive physiology, and impaired diffusion (DLCO).

### 13. Important Formula
$$ \text{Total Lung Capacity (TLC)} = \text{Vital Capacity (VC)} + \text{Residual Volume (RV)} $$

### 14. Mnemonics
- Obstructive Out, Restrictive In (Obstructive has trouble getting air OUT; Restrictive has trouble getting air IN).

### 15. Viva Questions
1. What is Residual Volume and how is it measured? **Answer**: The volume of air remaining in the lungs after maximal expiration. It cannot be measured by simple spirometry; it requires helium dilution or body plethysmography.
2. Explain the difference between a capacity and a volume. **Answer**: A capacity is the sum of two or more lung volumes (e.g., Functional Residual Capacity = Expiratory Reserve Volume + Residual Volume).

### 16. MCQs
1. A patient has an FEV1/FVC ratio of 55%. This is indicative of:
   * A) Restrictive lung disease
   * B) Obstructive lung disease
   * C) Normal lung function
   * D) Respiratory muscle weakness
   *Answer*: B (An FEV1/FVC ratio less than 70% defines an obstructive ventilatory defect.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient with dyspnea has an FVC of 60% predicted, an FEV1 of 62% predicted, and an FEV1/FVC ratio of 82%. **Analysis**: The normal FEV1/FVC ratio rules out obstruction. The low FVC suggests a restrictive pattern, which should be confirmed by measuring Total Lung Capacity (TLC < 80% confirms restriction).

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: What is the normal FEV1/FVC ratio in healthy adults?
  **Back**: Approximately 75% to 80% (or above 70%).
- **Front**: Which lung volume cannot be measured by spirometry?
  **Back**: Residual Volume (RV).

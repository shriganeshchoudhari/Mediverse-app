# Pregnancy & Placenta

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Pregnancy & Placenta.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
The placenta is like a specialized life-support pod docked onto the maternal ship: it serves as the fetus's lungs (gas exchange), kidneys (waste filtering), and digestive tract (nutrient intake) while releasing hormones (hCG) that modify the maternal cockpit settings to keep the portal open.

### 3. Basic Concept
Pregnancy involves major maternal cardiovascular, respiratory, and renal adaptations to support fetal development, mediated by placental hormones.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Uterus, decidua, placental villi (syncytiotrophoblast and cytotrophoblast layers), umbilical cord (2 arteries, 1 vein).

### 5. Physiology
Placenta secretes hCG (first trimester corpus luteum rescue), Progesterone (maintains uterine quiescence), and Estrogen (uterine growth). Maternal blood volume increases by 30-50%, and cardiac output rises by 30-40%.

### 6. Mechanism
Placental Gas Exchange: Umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta. Oxygen diffuses from maternal intervillous spaces into the umbilical vein driven by the partial pressure gradient and the higher oxygen affinity of fetal hemoglobin (HbF).

### 10. Clinical Correlation
- **Preeclampsia Diagnostics (ACOG Criteria)**: Preeeclampsia is defined as new-onset gestational hypertension (systolic $\ge 140$ mmHg or diastolic $\ge 90$ mmHg on two readings at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation) combined with **either** proteinuria ($\ge 300$ mg per 24-hour urine collection or protein-to-creatinine ratio $\ge 0.3$) **or** in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset maternal end-organ dysfunction (including thrombocytopenia $<100,000/\mu\text{L}$, renal insufficiency with serum creatinine $>1.1$ mg/dL, impaired liver function with transaminases elevated to twice normal, severe persistent right upper quadrant pain, pulmonary edema, or new-onset cerebral or visual disturbances). It is caused by abnormal cytotrophoblast remodeling of uterine spiral arteries, leading to high-resistance placental perfusion, chronic ischemia, and systemic endothelial dysfunction.

### 11. Disorders
1. Preeclampsia: Gestational hypertension combined with proteinuria or signs of end-organ damage (severe features).
2. Gestational Diabetes: Placental Lactogen (hPL) causes maternal insulin resistance, increasing glucose availability for the fetus.

### 15. Viva Questions
1. Why does physiological anemia of pregnancy occur? **Answer**: Plasma volume increases by 50% while RBC mass only increases by 20-30%, resulting in hemodilution.
2. Explain the shift in the maternal oxygen-hemoglobin curve during pregnancy. **Answer**: Maternal Hb-O2 curve shifts to the right (Bohr effect due to local fetal acidosis), facilitating oxygen release to the placenta.

### 16. MCQs
1. Which placental hormone is responsible for maintaining the corpus luteum during the first trimester?
   * A) Human Placental Lactogen (hPL)
   * B) Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)
   * C) Progesterone
   * D) Estriol
   *Answer*: B (hCG, secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast, prevents corpus luteum degradation, ensuring progesterone synthesis until the placenta takes over.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A pregnant woman at 32 weeks gestation presents with a BP of 150/95 mmHg, +2 protein on a urine dipstick, and persistent right upper quadrant pain. **Analysis**: Preeclampsia with severe features (indicated by right upper quadrant pain, reflecting hepatic congestion/stretch of Glisson's capsule). Abnormal cytotrophoblast invasion of spiral arteries caused placental ischemia, triggering systemic endothelial injury, vasoconstriction, plasma volume contraction, and glomerular leak. Preeclampsia is diagnosed based on new-onset hypertension after 20 weeks plus either proteinuria or severe features indicating end-organ dysfunction.

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: What is the normal vessel composition of the umbilical cord?
  **Back**: Two umbilical arteries (carrying deoxygenated blood to the placenta) and one umbilical vein (carrying oxygenated blood to the fetus).
- **Front**: How does GFR change during normal pregnancy?
  **Back**: It increases by 50% due to elevated renal blood flow.

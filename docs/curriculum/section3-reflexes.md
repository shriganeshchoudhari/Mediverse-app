# Spinal Reflexes

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Spinal Reflexes.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
A spinal reflex is like an emergency circuit breaker in a house: when current surges (a painful pinch or sudden muscle stretch), the local circuit trips immediately at the panel (spinal cord) to cut power, without waiting for signals from the main power company (the brain).

### 3. Basic Concept
Reflexes are involuntary, stereotyped responses to sensory stimuli. The reflex arc consists of: Receptor, Afferent nerve, Integration center (spinal cord), Efferrent nerve, and Effector organ.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Muscle spindles (detect muscle length), Golgi tendon organs (detect muscle tension), Ia/II afferent fibers, alpha/gamma motor neurons, and inhibitory interneurons in the spinal cord.

### 5. Physiology
Monosynaptic reflex (e.g. Stretch reflex) has no interneurons. Polysynaptic reflex (e.g. Withdrawal reflex) has one or more interneurons. Reciprocal inhibition prevents antagonist muscles from opposing the reflex.

### 6. Mechanism
Stretch Reflex (Knee Jerk): 1. Patellar tendon tap stretches quadriceps, 2. Muscle spindles detect stretch, Ia afferents fire, 3. Direct synapse with alpha motor neurons in spinal cord, 4. Quadriceps contract. Concurrently, Ia branch activates inhibitory interneurons to relax hamstrings.

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Hyperreflexia (clonus) indicates an Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) lesion due to loss of descending inhibition. Hyporeflexia indicates a Lower Motor Neuron (UMN) lesion (nerve root compression).

### 11. Disorders
1. Spinal Shock: Temporary loss of all reflex activity below spinal cord injury level. 2. Tetanus: Clostridium tetani toxin blocks glycine/GABA release from inhibitory interneurons, causing spastic paralysis.

### 15. Viva Questions
1. What is the difference between alpha and gamma motor neurons? **Answer**: Alpha motor neurons innervate extrafusal muscle fibers (generating force). Gamma motor neurons innervate intrafusal muscle fibers (adjusting spindle sensitivity).
1. What does the Golgi Tendon Organ detect? **Answer**: It detects muscle tension (preventing damage from excessive load via autogenic inhibition).

### 16. MCQs
1. Which of the following is a monosynaptic reflex?
   * A) Withdrawal reflex
   * B) Stretch reflex
   * C) Crossed extensor reflex
   * D) Inverse stretch reflex
   *Answer*: B (The stretch reflex (e.g. knee jerk) is the only monosynaptic reflex in the human body.)

2. The sensory receptor that detects muscle tension is the:
   * A) Pacinian corpuscle
   * B) Muscle spindle
   * C) Golgi tendon organ
   * D) Free nerve ending
   *Answer*: C (Golgi tendon organs are arranged in series with muscle fibers to monitor tension.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient with a history of stroke presents with brisk knee reflexes (+4) and spasticity in their right leg. **Analysis**: The stroke destroyed descending inhibitory tracts (pyramidal/corticospinal), leaving the spinal reflex arcs uninhibited and hyperactive.

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: Which afferent fiber type carries signals from the muscle spindle?
  **Back**: Ia (primary endings, dynamic) and II (secondary endings, static) fibers.
- **Front**: What is reciprocal inhibition?
  **Back**: The simultaneous contraction of an agonist muscle and relaxation of its antagonist during a reflex.

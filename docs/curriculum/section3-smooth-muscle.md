# Smooth Muscle

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Smooth Muscle.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
Unlike skeletal muscle's rapid toggle switch, smooth muscle contraction is like a dimmer switch: it is slower, sustained, uses less energy, and regulates tension in hollow organs (like blood vessels) over long periods without tiring.

### 3. Basic Concept
Smooth muscle lacks striations and troponin. Contraction is regulated by calcium-dependent myosin light chain phosphorylation, allowing sustained tone (latch state) with minimal ATP usage.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Spindle-shaped cells containing dense bodies (analogous to Z-lines), actin, myosin, and calmodulin. Lacks T-tubules; uses caveolae instead.

### 5. Physiology
Can be Multi-unit (independent fibers: ciliary muscle, iris, piloerector) or Unitary/Single-unit (syncytial, connected by gap junctions: gut, uterus, blood vessels).

### 6. Mechanism
Contraction: 1. Ca2+ enters cell via L-type channels or IP3 pathways, 2. Ca2+ binds to Calmodulin, 3. Ca2+-Calmodulin complex activates Myosin Light Chain Kinase (MLCK), 4. MLCK phosphorylates myosin light chain, 5. Myosin ATPase activity increases, cross-bridge cycling occurs. Relaxation requires Myosin Phosphatase.

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Amlodipine) block L-type Ca2+ channels in vascular smooth muscle, causing vasodilation to treat hypertension.

### 11. Disorders
1. Achalasia: Failure of lower esophageal sphincter smooth muscle to relax. 2. Asthma: Hyper-responsiveness and spasm of bronchial smooth muscle.

### 14. Mnemonics
- MLCK Kicks it on, MLCP Pulls it off (MLCK phosphorylates to contract, MLCP dephosphorylates to relax).

### 15. Viva Questions
1. How does smooth muscle maintain tension with low ATP usage? **Answer**: Via the 'latch-bridge' mechanism, where dephosphorylated myosin remains attached to actin for prolonged periods.
1. What is the smooth muscle equivalent of Troponin C? **Answer**: Calmodulin, which binds calcium to initiate the regulatory cascade.

### 16. MCQs
1. Which enzyme is directly responsible for smooth muscle relaxation?
   * A) Myosin light chain kinase
   * B) Myosin light chain phosphatase
   * C) Adenylate cyclase
   * D) Calmodulin
   *Answer*: B (Myosin light chain phosphatase dephosphorylates myosin, leading to detachment and relaxation.)

2. Where are actin filaments anchored in smooth muscle cells?
   * A) Z-discs
   * B) Dense bodies
   * C) Caveolae
   * D) Sarcoplasmic reticulum
   *Answer*: B (Dense bodies act as the structural anchors for actin in smooth muscle, similar to Z-discs in striated muscle.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient with high blood pressure is prescribed Nifedipine. They experience flushing and ankle swelling due to arteriole dilation. **Analysis**: Nifedipine blocks voltage-gated L-type Ca2+ channels in arterial smooth muscle, decreasing Ca2+-Calmodulin activation of MLCK, leading to relaxation.

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: Does smooth muscle contain troponin?
  **Back**: No, it uses calmodulin for calcium sensing instead.
- **Front**: What is the difference between multi-unit and unitary smooth muscle?
  **Back**: Unitary acts as a single syncytial unit connected by gap junctions; multi-unit fibers act independently.

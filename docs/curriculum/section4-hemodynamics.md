# Hemodynamics

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Hemodynamics.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
Blood vessels are like a city's plumbing system: narrow pipes (arterioles) offer high resistance, wide pipes (aorta) allow high flow, and a viscous fluid (like syrup vs. water) requires much more pump pressure to move through the network.

### 3. Basic Concept
Hemodynamics is the study of the physical laws governing blood flow, pressure, and resistance. Blood flow (Q) is directly proportional to the pressure gradient (ΔP) and inversely proportional to vascular resistance (R).

### 4. Anatomy Review
The vascular tree consists of the high-pressure arterial system (aorta, arteries, arterioles), the exchange network (capillaries), and the low-pressure venous system (venules, veins, vena cava). Arterioles contain smooth muscle and serve as the main resistance vessels.

### 5. Physiology
Normal pressures: Aorta SBP/DBP = 120/80 mmHg; Capillaries = 30 mmHg (arterial end) to 10 mmHg (venous end); Vena Cava = 2-4 mmHg. Blood flow is laminar under normal conditions but can become turbulent when velocity increases (high Reynolds number).

### 6. Mechanism
According to Poiseuille's law, resistance is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the radius ($R \propto 1/r^4$). Vasoconstriction (radius halved) increases resistance 16-fold, reducing blood flow. Viscosity ($\eta$), driven by hematocrit, and vessel length ($L$) also increase resistance.

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Turbulent flow produces sounds (bruits in constricted arteries, murmurs in stenotic heart valves) that can be auscultated. It is predicted by the Reynolds number ($Re > 2000-3000$).

### 11. Disorders
1. Atherosclerosis: Narrowing of arteries, increasing resistance and arterial pressure. 2. Polycythemia: Elevated RBC count, increasing viscosity and workload on the heart.

### 13. Important Formula
$$ Q = \frac{\Delta P}{R}, \quad R = \frac{8\eta L}{\pi r^4}, \quad Re = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta} $$

### 15. Viva Questions
1. What is Poiseuille's law? **Answer**: It defines resistance in laminar flow: $R = 8\eta L / \pi r^4$, showing that radius is the most powerful determinant.
2. Explain the difference between laminar and turbulent flow. **Answer**: Laminar flow is silent and layered (fastest in the center). Turbulent flow is chaotic, noisy, and requires higher pressure to drive.

### 16. MCQs
1. If the radius of an arteriole is reduced to half its original size, the resistance to blood flow increases by:
   * A) 2 times
   * B) 4 times
   * C) 8 times
   * D) 16 times
   *Answer*: D (According to Poiseuille's law, resistance is inversely proportional to $r^4$. $(1/0.5)^4 = 16$.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient presents with a carotid bruit. Doppler ultrasound reveals 70% narrowing of the internal carotid artery. **Analysis**: The stenosis reduces vessel radius, causing a local increase in blood velocity ($v$) and turbulence ($Re > 2000$), generating the auscultable bruit.

# Gas Exchange

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of Gas Exchange.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
Gas exchange across the respiratory membrane is like commuters passing through a turnstile: the rate of diffusion depends on the area of the turnstile (alveolar surface area), the pressure pushing from behind (partial pressure gradient), and how thin the gate is (membrane thickness).

### 3. Basic Concept
Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide transfer between alveoli and pulmonary capillaries via simple passive diffusion, governed by Fick's law of diffusion.

### 4. Anatomy Review
The respiratory membrane: alveolar epithelium, epithelial basement membrane, interstitial space, capillary basement membrane, and capillary endothelium. Total thickness is <0.6 µm.

### 5. Physiology
Normal partial pressures: Alveolar pO2 = 104 mmHg, pCO2 = 40 mmHg. Mixed venous pO2 = 40 mmHg, pCO2 = 45 mmHg. The diffusion coefficient of CO2 is 20 times higher than that of O2.

### 6. Mechanism
Fick's Law of Diffusion: The volume of gas diffusing per minute ($V_{gas}$) is directly proportional to the surface area ($A$), diffusion coefficient ($D$), and partial pressure difference ($P_1 - P_2$), and inversely proportional to membrane thickness ($T$).

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Pulmonary edema increases membrane thickness ($T$), slowing O2 diffusion and causing arterial hypoxemia. Emphysema destroys alveolar walls, reducing surface area ($A$) and impairing exchange.

### 11. Disorders
1. Pulmonary Fibrosis: Thickening of the interstitial space, limiting gas diffusion. 2. Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) Mismatch: Shunt (V/Q = 0) or Dead Space (V/Q = infinity) impairing blood oxygenation.

### 14. Mnemonics
- CO2 is 20x faster (Carbon dioxide is 20 times more soluble than oxygen, diffusing rapidly despite a small pressure gradient).

### 15. Viva Questions
1. State Fick's Law of Diffusion. **Answer**: The diffusion rate is directly proportional to the surface area and pressure gradient, and inversely proportional to the membrane thickness.
2. What is the Alveolar-arterial (A-a) oxygen gradient? **Answer**: The difference between alveolar pO2 and arterial pO2. A high gradient (>15-20 mmHg) indicates intrinsic lung disease.

### 16. MCQs
1. Which of the following increases the rate of gas diffusion across the respiratory membrane?
   * A) Increased membrane thickness
   * B) Decreased alveolar surface area
   * C) Increased partial pressure gradient
   * D) Decreased gas solubility
   *Answer*: C (According to Fick's law, diffusion is directly proportional to the partial pressure gradient.)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient with pulmonary fibrosis presents with dyspnea. Arterial blood gas shows PaO2 of 60 mmHg and PaCO2 of 38 mmHg. **Analysis**: Fibrosis increases membrane thickness, limiting oxygen diffusion. CO2 remains normal because of its high diffusion coefficient.

### 18. Flashcards
- **Front**: What is the normal thickness of the respiratory membrane?
  **Back**: Less than 0.6 micrometers (typically 0.2 to 0.5 µm).
- **Front**: What is a pulmonary shunt?
  **Back**: Perfusion of alveoli that are not ventilated (V/Q = 0), preventing blood oxygenation.

# EEG & Sleep

### 1. Introduction
This chapter covers the physiological principles of EEG & Sleep.

### 2. Daily Life Analogy
Brain waves during sleep are like the noise of a city: when awake and active, the noise is rapid, random, and high-frequency (desynchronized beta waves); during deep sleep, the city synchronizes as everyone starts clapping together slowly, producing large, slow rhythmic beats (synchronized delta waves).

### 3. Basic Concept
The Electroencephalogram (EEG) records cortical postsynaptic potentials. Sleep is an active, highly regulated cyclical process consisting of NREM and REM stages.

### 4. Anatomy Review
Thalamus (pacemaker for cortical rhythmicity), reticular activating system (RAS, maintaining wakefulness), and suprachiasmatic nucleus (circadian clock).

### 5. Physiology
EEG waves: Alpha (awake, relaxed, 8-13 Hz); Beta (awake, alert/focused, 14-30 Hz); Theta (light sleep, 4-7 Hz); Delta (deep sleep, <4 Hz). Sleep cycle repeats every 90-110 minutes, shifting from NREM (75%) to REM (25%).

### 6. Mechanism
Sleep Cycle progression: 1. N1 (Theta waves), 2. N2 (Sleep Spindles and K-complexes), 3. N3 (Slow-Wave Sleep, Delta waves, growth hormone released), 4. REM (Desynchronized EEG resembling awake state, dreaming, muscle atonia, rapid eye movements).

### 10. Clinical Correlation
Narcolepsy is characterized by sudden, uncontrollable entry into REM sleep directly from wakefulness, caused by a loss of orexin (hypocretin) producing neurons in the lateral hypothalamus.

### 11. Disorders
1. Narcolepsy: Sleep attacks and cataplexy. 2. Somnambulism (Sleepwalking): Occurs during Stage N3 deep sleep, not during REM. 3. Insomnia: Difficulty falling or staying asleep.

### 13. Important Formula
$$ \text{Total Sleep Cycle Duration} = \text{N1} + \text{N2} + \text{N3} + \text{REM} \approx 90-100 \text{ minutes} $$

### 14. Mnemonics
- At Night, Sleep Descends (EEG waves from alert to deep sleep: **A**lpha -> **N**2 [Spindles] -> **S**low wave [Theta/N1] -> **D**elta). Alternatively: BATS Drink Blood (Alert awake = **B**eta; Relaxed awake = **A**lpha; N1 = **T**heta; N2 = **S**pindles; N3 = **D**elta; REM = **B**eta).

### 15. Viva Questions
1. What are sleep spindles and K-complexes, and where do they occur? **Answer**: Rhythmic bursts of activity (spindles) and high-voltage slow waves (K-complexes) diagnostic of Stage N2 NREM sleep.
2. Why is REM sleep called 'paradoxical sleep'? **Answer**: Because the EEG is highly active, resembling an alert awake state, yet the person is asleep and their skeletal muscles are completely paralyzed (atonia).

### 16. MCQs
1. Which sleep stage is characterized by Delta waves on the EEG?
   * A) Stage N1
   * B) Stage N2
   * C) Stage N3
   * D) REM sleep
   *Answer*: C (Stage N3 (Slow-wave sleep) is characterized by high-voltage, low-frequency delta waves (<4 Hz).)

### 17. Case-Based Learning
A patient experiences sudden loss of muscle tone when laughing (cataplexy) and falls asleep during meetings. Spinal tap reveals low hypocretin-1. **Analysis**: Narcolepsy. Hypocretin/orexin stabilization is lost, causing sudden, abnormal transitions directly into REM sleep.

### 18. Flashcards

- **Front**: During which sleep stage does sleepwalking occur?
  **Back**: Stage N3 NREM (slow-wave sleep).

# Early Clinical Exposure: Basic 12-Lead ECG Interpretation

## 1. Introduction
The Electrocardiogram (ECG) is a recording of the electrical activity of the heart over time, captured by electrodes placed on the skin. A standard ECG utilizes 12 viewpoints (leads) to map cardiac depolarization and repolarization.

---

## 2. Lead Placements & Viewpoints
*   **Limb Leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF)**: Map electrical vectors in the frontal plane.
*   **Chest Leads (V1 to V6)**: Map vectors in the horizontal (transverse) plane.
    *   **V1-V2**: Septal view.
    *   **V3-V4**: Anterior view.
    *   **V5-V6**: Lateral view.

---

## 3. Waveform Meanings
*   **P Wave**: Atrial depolarization.
*   **QRS Complex**: Ventricular depolarization (atrial repolarization is masked).
*   **T Wave**: Ventricular repolarization.
*   **PR Interval**: Time delay in the AV node (normally 0.12 to 0.20 seconds).

---

## 4. Heart Rate Calculations
For a regular rhythm:
$$\text{Heart Rate} = \frac{300}{\text{Number of large squares between consecutive R waves}}$$
Alternatively:
$$\text{Heart Rate} = \frac{1500}{\text{Number of small squares between consecutive R waves}}$$

---

## 5. Practice Viva Questions
1.  **Which lead represents the septal view of the heart?**
    *Answer:* Leads V1 and V2.
2.  **What does a prolonged PR interval (> 0.20s) indicate?**
    *Answer:* First-degree AV nodal block.
